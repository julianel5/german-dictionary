# English → German common-vocabulary coverage study (curated lemmas)

Complementing the raw-entry pilot ([`coverage-study.md`](coverage-study.md)), this
study answers the coverage question the pilot could not: **do the candidate
sources cover the words a person would actually look up** in an English → German
dictionary? The pilot measured a uniform sample of *raw* en.wiktionary entries
and found it dominated by rare/technical/form-of words (German coverage 5.83%).
This study measures coverage over a **curated list of common English lemmas** and
reports it **per source** and **per vocabulary category**.

> **Status: measured.** Frame **option A** (Wiktionary TV/movie 2006) was approved
> and the measurement ran on 2026-10-08 against the external pilot artefacts. All
> metrics below are `MEASURED`. No external data was imported, no schema was
> changed, no migration was created, and no API/UI behaviour was modified.

Companion documents: [`pilot-readiness.md`](pilot-readiness.md),
[`source-register.md`](source-register.md), [`coverage-study.md`](coverage-study.md),
[`plan.md`](plan.md).

## 0. Scope

- **Question.** For a fixed, non-cherry-picked list of **common** English lemmas
  (500–1000), how completely and how faithfully can en.wiktionary (via
  Wiktextract), de.wiktionary and FreeDict eng-deu supply: the lemma, its
  definition, **German translation equivalents** (primary), German
  gender/inflection enrichment, examples, and what is the **exclusive
  contribution** of each source?
- **Out of scope.** Full import; schema/migration; API/UI behaviour; search;
  anything that changes the application. Same guardrails as the pilot: read-only,
  raw artefacts never committed, nothing measured is guessed.
- **Task separation.** *Curation* (which words are "common") and *measurement*
  (what the sources contain for them) are separate steps with separate scripts,
  so the choice of frame cannot sway the measurement.

## 1. Sampling frame: "common English words"

### 1.1 Why a frame is needed

The raw English lexicon contains over a million entries, most of them rare. A
sample from the raw set is therefore not a sample of what users look up. To fix
the population we need an external, attributable frequency/common-word signal —
the repository has **none** (the `fixtures/frequency.json` corpus
`fixture-de-2026` is a hand-authored synthetic fixture, and kaikki data carries
no frequency). `plan.md` §9 (R5) keeps real frequency an open gap.

### 1.2 Requirements on the frame

1. **Verifiable provenance** — exact URL (or repo commit), retrieval date, and a
   machine-readable hash recorded in the generated list's header.
2. **Adequate licence** — must permit its use inside a CC BY-SA derived pipeline
   and the creation of derived metadata; the frame itself is *not* redistributed
   (see §5), so the obligation is restricted to derivation.
3. **Not fabricated** — the word list must come from a published, reproducible
   source, never invented locally (per milestone instructions).
4. **Size headroom** — after the documented filters (§2) must leave ≥500 lemmas;
   the target final size is **500–1000**.

### 1.3 Candidates and the approval gate

The milestone instructions require **approval before downloading any new
source**. Three candidates satisfy the requirements to different degrees:

| option | source | licence | format / size | pros | cons |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A (recommended)** | Wiktionary "Frequency lists", TV & movie scripts (2006) — `Wiktionary:Frequency_lists/TV/2006/…` (sub-pages, e.g. `1-10000`), hosted on **en.wiktionary** | **CC BY-SA 4.0** (the en.wiktionary licence; the list *is* a wiktionary page) | wikitext page, tens of KB; orthographic tokens with counts (per billion) | same project+licence as the primary data source; provenance = permalink/revision (`oldid`); the page itself documents coverage (top 1,000 ≈ 85.5%, top 10,000 ≈ 97.2%) | tokens, not lemmas (filters §2 handle forms); 2006 corpus (stale counts, structure still indicative) |
| **B** | hermitdave/FrequencyWords — `content/2018/en/en_50k.txt` (OpenSubtitles-based) | **CC BY-SA 4.0** for the data (MIT for code) | flat `word <space> count`, ~2–5 MB | clean machine-readable format; current corpus (2018) | third-party mirror; larger file; same CC BY-SA obligation as A |
| **C** | no download — expert-curated list (~600 words) under a documented rubric (semantic domains × grammatical classes, all lemmas attested in en.wiktionary) | our own curation (no source text copied) | a few KB generated by a script | zero licensing work; immediately executable | **not** corpus-ranked; explicitly non-representative; the words are "common by rubric", not by usage evidence |

The decision is a milestone input; this document does not choose silently.
Until a frame is approved, §10 metrics stay `PENDING`. **No other frequency
source is under consideration** (Leipzig/Kelly/SUBTLEX/NGC lists have
restrictive or unclear terms, or would be redundant given A/B).

## 2. Inclusion criteria (documented filters, applied by the generator)

The generator (`scripts/import/generate_common_lemmas.py`, proc
`generate-common-lemmas/1.0`) draws the tokens of the approved frame, from the
most frequent downward, and applies **all** of these filters in order:

| # | criterion | purpose (milestone task 2) |
|---|---|---|
| F0 | **Skip struck rows** — rows marked `<s>` in the source are page glitches, not words | removes extraction garbage (rank 840 `nbsp`) |
| F1 | **Single orthographic token** — reject any token containing whitespace | multi-word expressions are a separate concern (§4.4) |
| F2 | **Alphabetic surface** — reject tokens with `-`/`'` in leading/trailing position, digits, or any other non-`[a-zA-Z]` character. A **single intra-word apostrophe is allowed**: contractions such as `don't`, `I'm`, `won't` are lexemes with their own en.wiktionary entries and land in the `function` category at measurement | removes symbols, suffix-like junk and extraction artifacts without dropping productive contractions |
| F5 | **Cap** — take the first `--limit` surviving tokens | task size 500–1000; record how many were filtered |

**Inflected forms and POS assignability are not pre-filtered.** F3 (a token is an
**inflected-only** form when it appears solely as the inflected form of another
lemma, with no headword record of its own) and F4 (POS assignable) are
**measured** against the en.wiktionary extract by `common_coverage.py`, which
reports them as `lemmas_inflected_only_nonheadword` / `lemmas_missing_from_en` —
never guessed by the generator. In the measured run both were **0**, so within
this frame the inflected forms are dictionary headwords in their own right
(e.g. `went`, `said`, `thought`). The filters are *curation*, applied once; the
measurement script accepts whatever list it is given, so a filtering mistake is
visible in the report, not hidden.

## 3. Categories and POS handling

Category tags attached by the generator; when absent, the measurement derives
them from the data's own POS (`category_source = "derived"`):

| tag | meaning |
|---|---|
| `noun` | en.wiktionary POS `noun` |
| `verb` | POS `verb`; the measurement also labels verbs `irregular` (record or form carries an `irregular` tag), `conjugated` (inflected past/participle attested, no `irregular` tag) or `minimal` (no inflected forms) — **never inferred beyond the tags** |
| `adj` | POS `adj` |
| `adv` | POS `adv` |
| `function` | closed-class POS: determiner, pronoun, preposition, conjunction, article, particle, numeral, interjection, contraction |
| `other` | proper nouns, names and anything outside the above |

The functional-word category is naturally dense in frequency frames (the, of, and,
to, a, in, for, is, on, that, with, …), which is exactly the product target; the
raw-entry pilot could not measure it at all.

## 4. Identity: homonyms, variants, polysemy, multi-word expressions

**No automatic merging anywhere** (milestone task 3; consistent with
`pilot-readiness.md` §3 and `plan.md` §4.3):

| concern | rule |
|---|---|
| **Homonyms** | a lemma is matched to **all** records `(word, pos, etymology_number)` with that spelling; each record is analysed and kept **separate** (`identity` key); lemma-level figures are unions over its records, and the per-record detail is in the report — two *bank*s are never one entry |
| **Orthographic variants** | matching is `casefold`-only; surface forms stay the source's (British/American spellings remain distinct lemmas); no stemming, no "same word" guess |
| **Polysemy** | measured (sense counts per record), not enforced or merged; sense→translation association is reported exactly as in the pilot (structural: sense-level translations are absent in this data) |
| **Multi-word expressions** | **excluded from the primary frame** (F1) and documented as a follow-up measurement; single-token lemmas are the target vocabulary for this milestone |

## 5. Curated list format, provenance and repository policy

The generated list is a UTF-8 text file (default
`data/processed/common-lemmas.tsv`, gitignored like all of `data/processed/`):

```
# generate-common-lemmas/1.0
# frame: Wiktionary:Frequency lists/TV/2006/1-1000 (en.wiktionary)
# url: https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Wiktionary:Frequency_lists/TV/2006/1-1000
# source_revid: 82449797
# source_rev_timestamp: 2024-10-21T05:33:50Z
# licence: CC BY-SA 4.0 (contents of en.wiktionary)
# retrieved: 2026-10-08T19:38Z
# source_sha256: 5397e46a…
# rows: 2017  struck_F0: 1  surface_F2: 1  beyond_limit_F5: 0  duplicates_dropped: 1  lemmas: 997
# category: derived from the en.wiktionary extract at measurement time
# columns: word | category (empty; derived) | rank:N (frame rank)
house\t\trank:253
go\t\trank:57
…
```

Rules:

- Lines starting with `#` are provenance; the measurement script records the
  header verbatim in its report, so every metric carries its frame.
- Row columns are optional: `word`, `word<TAB>category`, or
  `word<TAB>[category]<TAB>rank:N` — the category is left empty in the generated
  list and **derived from the data's own POS at measurement time**
  (`category_source: "derived"`); classification is never invented here.
- **Nothing derived from the frame is committed to git.** The list file, the
  measurement report and the per-lemma detail live in `data/processed/` (or the
  external working directory) and are not committed; **the repository receives
  only the generator + measurement scripts and the aggregate metrics in this
  document** — no source text is copied into fixtures or the repo (data policy:
  `data/raw/README.md`, milestone task 7).
- Categories are optional per line (`word` alone ⇒ derived from data).

## 6. Measurement

Reuses the local artefacts and code from the pilot — nothing is re-downloaded
(milestone task 4: the en/de JSONL and the FreeDict TEI live in the external
pilot working directory):

```
python -m scripts.import.common_coverage \
  --en       <external>/en/raw-wiktextract-data.jsonl.gz \
  --de       <external>/de/raw-wiktextract-data.jsonl.gz \
  --freedict <external>/freedict/extracted/eng-deu/eng-deu.tei \
  --lemmas   data/processed/common-lemmas.tsv \
  --report   data/processed/common-words-coverage.json
```

Implemented in `scripts/import/common_coverage.py` (procedure
`common-words-coverage/1.0`), reusing the pilot's streaming, German-equivalent,
de-index and FreeDict-TEI logic. It:

1. reads and dedupes the lemma list (header recorded);
2. streams the en artefact once, capturing every record per lemma
   (homonyms separate) and detecting `inflected_only` tokens;
3. analyses each record with the pilot's factual analysis (definitions,
   German equivalents, senses, examples) and aggregates per lemma;
4. streams the de artefact for the observed German equivalents (gender, forms,
   glosses);
5. streams the FreeDict TEI for the lemma headwords (equivalents, gender);
6. writes `data/processed/common-words-coverage.json` with scan stats, per-lemma
   detail, defect classes, and the metrics below.

### 6.1 Metrics (names match the report JSON)

Per source and per category (and, for the whole list, per observed POS):

| metric | definition |
|---|---|
| `lemmas_scored` | list size actually measured |
| `en_headword_present` | lemma has ≥1 record with `lang_code == en` |
| `en_de_coverage` | lemma carries ≥1 German translation (`code == de`) in en.wiktionary |
| `freedict_headword_present` | exact headword present in FreeDict |
| `freedict_de_coverage` | ≥1 German equivalent from FreeDict |
| `any_source_de_coverage` | German equivalent from **either** source |
| `definition_coverage` | ≥1 English gloss in en.wiktionary |
| `multiple_german_equivalents` | ≥2 distinct German surfaces (casefolded) |
| `de_enrichment_matched` / `gender` / `forms` / `gloss` | matched German equivalents in the de edition and the share carrying gender/forms/a German gloss |
| `example_coverage` / `example_translation_coverage` | examples present; the share carrying a translation (the raw data has none — structural) |

### 6.2 Exclusive contribution (source comparison, milestone task 5)

Each lemma gets one bucket; counts and percentages are reported globally and
per category:

| bucket | meaning |
|---|---|
| `both` | German from en.wiktionary **and** FreeDict |
| `en_only` | German only from en.wiktionary |
| `freedict_only` | German only from FreeDict (**FreeDict's exclusive, additive value**) |
| `neither` | no German from either source — the true coverage gap for common words |

Plus defect classes carried over from the pilot (never silently dropped):
`no_german_translation`, `sense_unmapped`, `sense_text_unmatched`,
`word_field_absent`, `duplicate_translation`, `invalid_record`, plus
`missing_record`, and the `inflected_only` counter.

## 7. Framework for the recommendation (set after measurement)

- If `en_de_coverage` on common words is high (~≥70%), en.wiktionary is
  sufficient as the **MVP translation source**; if it is mid (30–70%), FreeDict's
  `freedict_only` share decides whether it earns its copyleft+merge cost; if it
  is low, the product needs a **different translation source** entirely.
- de.wiktionary enrichment value = `de_enrichment_*` shares on matched German
  equivalents.
- `neither`/`freedict_only`/`en_only` at category level say *where* the gaps are
  (functional words? verbs?) and which source to fix them first.
- All comparisons are **within the curated frame** and are never presented as
  whole-language coverage (§8).

## 8. Limitations (stated, never papered over)

1. **Curated frame ≠ language.** Every coverage figure applies to the selected
   common-word list and its frame/corpus; no claim of whole-language
   coverage is made from it (milestone task 6).
2. **Frame age/bias.** The TV/movie-scripts corpus skews conversational (option
   A) — by design for "what people look up", but not academic/technical
   vocabulary.
3. **Homonym aggregation.** Lemma-level "has German" is a union over homonymous
   records; per-record detail is authoritative and reported.
4. **Token ≠ lemma in the frame.** Frequency frames are token lists; the
   `inflected_only` classifier and the per-lemma detail bound this — in this
   frame every filtered rank surface is a dictionary headword — for other frames
   they do not eliminate it.
5. **De-edition matching** is an exact casefold match on surface; German
   equivalents without a de.wiktionary entry are simply not enriched (never
   inferred).
6. **Licences (unchanged blockers):** L1 (kaikki JSONL file licence)** unverified,
   L3 (CC BY-SA vs GPLv3/AGPLv3 in a shared database)** open — they block
   committing/shipping derived data regardless of measured coverage.

## 9. Inputs and integrity

The measurement consumes the **already-verified** artefacts of the pilot
(`coverage-study.md` §2); the only new input is the curated list, whose hash is
recorded in its own header and in the report's `inputs.lemmas.sha256`.

## 11. Recommendation

Applying §7 to the measured numbers:

1. **en.wiktionary is sufficient as the MVP translation source for common,
   English→German vocabulary.** It delivers a German equivalent for **79.5%** of
   the 997 common lemmas (93.2% of nouns, 89.2% of adjectives, 90.8% of adverbs),
   **100% definitions**, and **94% examples**, all from one CC BY-SA source with
   aligned sense numbering (`sense_unmapped`/`sense_text_unmatched` counts above
   are the measure of its roughness, most visible on polysemous verbs).
2. **FreeDict's additive value is real but narrow and best deferred past a
   licence decision.** It is the *only* German source for **11.9%** of common
   lemmas (119) and raises combined equivalence coverage to **91.5%**; its
   weakest/useful-stretch category is exactly the hardest for en.wiktionary:
   verbs (FreeDict 69.6% vs en 54.4%). But it is **NO-GO to merge or redistribute**
   until **L3** (CC BY-SA vs GPLv3/AGPLv3) is legally cleared — the measured
   coverage does **not** change that.
3. **The real gap is functional words, verbs and the `other` bucket.**
   85 lemmas (8.5%) have German from **neither** source; the categories with the
   worst en-only coverage are exactly verbs (54.4%), function words (60.6%) and
   `other` (73.4%). Any follow-up source should target those.
4. **German enrichment via de.wiktionary is worth it at import time**: gender on
   43.6% of matched nouns, forms on 86.4%, German gloss on 99.6% — a strong
   baseline for German-side display; gender coverage is partial, not
   comprehensive.
5. **Example translations cannot come from this data** (structural gap, §6.1);
   countable elsewhere only (0.52% here).
